# Entrance Music Research — Survey Notes

Source doc: *Wrestling Entrance Music History and Licensing* (Google Drive, fileId `1E83ogt05TsqADEjuSVzvy1WxNRgvt-wVddFy4jgRQVk`).
Surveyed 2026-04-27.

The doc has four tabs: Tab 1 longform, Tab 2 1960s–70s exceptions, Tab 3 modern licensing, Tab 4 historical licensing. Citation footnotes 1–41 lean heavily on Wikipedia, prowrestlingstories.com, fandom wikis, and Reddit threads. Below: things to verify before publishing, anecdotes that can carry weekly short-form pieces, and gaps worth filling.

## Factual claims to verify before publishing

These are claims that anchor the longform's argument; if any is wrong, it shifts the piece. None are clearly wrong — most are restating well-known wrestling lore — but the citations behind them are secondary.

1. **Lord Patrick Lansdowne, May 30, 1935, Toledo Civic Auditorium, vs. Johnny Stote.** Cite is `prowrestlinghistory.com/ohio/toledo/toledo-local.pdf`. Worth confirming against the *Toledo Blade* or local wrestling program archives. The 1935 date matters because it's the doc's foundation for "music before TV."

2. **Lansdowne playing "God Save the King" via live band.** Sole source is prowrestlingstories.com (footnote 1). This is the kind of color detail that often originates from a single oral history and propagates uncited. Look for period match programs or contemporary press.

3. **Mildred Burke "first million-dollar female athlete in history."** Sourced to a Fort Lauderdale Film Festival program guide (footnote 8). That's a weak source for an absolute claim. Jeff Leen's *The Queen of the Ring* (in our bibliography) is the right primary cite.

4. **Gorgeous George origin night, Eugene, Oregon, 1941, "Isn't he gorgeous?".** John Capouya's *Gorgeous George: The Bizarre Life of a Wrestling Legend* (2008) is the definitive source. Different sources give 1941 vs 1942 vs 1943. The "Eugene" vs "Portland" detail also varies. Capouya goes with 1941–42 transition.

5. **Gorgeous George first TV appearance November 11, 1947.** Confirmable via Capouya or period TV listings.

6. **George commanding 50% of the gate by the 1950s.** Verify via Capouya. Common figure but high.

7. **"Real American" written 1984, originally for U.S. Express, used by them October 1985, transferred to Hogan in December 1985.** Common timeline that Wikipedia and Reddit both cite, but original source is murkier. Rick Derringer's own 2022 Reddit comments (footnote 34) align. Verify the December 1985 handoff date — many sources say January 1986, around WrestleMania 2 lead-in.

8. **Jimmy Hart's pre-wrestling career — The Gentrys, "Keep On Dancing" 1965, "sold over a million."** Verify million-seller claim; could be RIAA Gold (500k) being misremembered.

9. **Sgt. Slaughter "first to use entrance music in WWWF" with Marines' Hymn "during the late 1970s."** Slaughter's WWWF/WWF debut was 1980, not the late 70s. He worked Mid-Atlantic before that. The Marines' Hymn at MSG was probably an early-80s thing. Date-window probably wrong as stated.

10. **Bruiser Brody / "Immigrant Song" "in the late 1970s, particularly in Japan."** Brody's first AJPW tour was 1979. Verify the date he started using Led Zeppelin. AJPW television footage from the era should confirm.

11. **Leroy Brown / "Bad, Bad Leroy Brown" at the Olympic Auditorium, LA, 1970s.** The Olympic was NWA Hollywood territory. "Historians note this was considered unusual in that era" — sourced to footnote 11 (Reddit thread). Find a wrestler-historian statement on the record.

12. **Buddy Rogers — live musicians transposing "Nature Boy" for entrances in the 1960s.** This is the foundation of the Rogers→Flair "Nature Boy" lineage. Solid lore but the "live musicians" detail needs a primary source.

13. **Mil Máscaras / Jigsaw's "Sky High," AJPW, 1975, "iconic theme that was a hit twice over in Japan."** "Sky High" was a 1975 UK hit and a Japan hit (partly via the wrestling association). The chronology is plausible. Verify start date in AJPW.

14. **Big Daddy entrance to The Seekers' "We Shall Not Be Moved."** Big Daddy was Shirley Crabtree, Joint Promotions. The Seekers' version of the song should be confirmable; if it's actually a different recording the attribution flips.

15. **Adrian Street / "Imagine What I Could Do To You" in the 1970s.** Adrian Street recorded his own album *Shake, Wrestle 'n' Roll* (1986). Verify whether the entrance song predates that album.

16. **Papa Berg, Inc. v. WWE — July 2012 filing, settled out of court 2014, Northern District of Texas.** Court records are reachable. The doc cites Scribd and the NDTex blog. The actual docket number is `3:12-cv-02406-B` — pull the PACER summary if needed.

17. **"Eat Your Heart Out, Rick Springfield" credited to Jimmy Hart on *The Wrestling Album* (1985).** Verify against the album credits — the project had multiple writers/producers.

18. **Bruno Sammartino "without a single note of music" throughout his career.** Reasonable claim but absolute. Verify there were no MSG specials or special-event entrances with music.

## Anecdotes for the weekly short-form slot

Each of these is a self-contained story that can carry a 200–400 word post. Numbered for reference; the order is by historical chronology, not priority.

1. **Lansdowne and "God Save the King."** First documented use of a national anthem to manufacture heel heat — a foppish British aristocrat using the actual British anthem to provoke American crowds. (Verify per #1, #2 above.)

2. **Mildred Burke's carnival-band distractions.** Music as a tool to cover trouble with a "mark" who'd figured out the work — repurposed later into regal entrance music to legitimize her championship. The pivot is the story.

3. **The "Isn't he gorgeous?" night in Eugene.** George Wagner overhearing the comment backstage and rebuilding his entire act around it. (Verify per #4.)

4. **November 11, 1947 — Gorgeous George on TV.** The first televised entrance-as-spectacle and how it made him a national celebrity overnight.

5. **The Chanel #10 sterilization ritual.** George's valet spraying the referee's hands before the official could check him for foreign objects. Establishes the "cowardly heel" template that ran for the next forty years.

6. **Buddy Rogers and the live musicians.** Rogers cueing live arena bands to transpose "Nature Boy" — the foundational moment of a song that would become Ric Flair's identity. (Verify per #12.)

7. **Big Daddy, "We Shall Not Be Moved," and the singalong.** UK crowds singing the chorus along with him. The participatory entrance — fans as part of the music — long before US wrestling figured this out. (Verify per #14.)

8. **Mil Máscaras and "Sky High."** The Japanese television director's choice that turned a UK pop song into a wrestling anthem; Jigsaw's track became a hit in Japan twice over because of the wrestling connection. (Verify per #13.)

9. **Bruiser Brody / "Immigrant Song."** The American outlaw using Led Zeppelin to terrorize AJPW arenas. The song fit Brody better than anything could have. (Verify per #10.)

10. **Sgt. Slaughter, Marines' Hymn, MSG.** Patriotic spectacle as entrance — the precursor to "Real American" by half a decade. (Verify per #9; date window probably wrong.)

11. **Leroy Brown at the Olympic.** Using a Jim Croce hit as your entrance music in the 1970s when nobody else in your territory did. (Verify per #11.)

12. **Titanes en el Ring, 1962.** The Argentine promotion that gave a unique theme song to *every* wrestler on the roster — twenty-plus years before WWF did the same — and released the themes as actual hit albums.

13. **The Wimpbusters parody.** Jerry Lawler's *Ghostbusters*-spoof music video mocking Jimmy Hart's First Family in Memphis. Music as angle, not just intro.

14. **"Badstreet USA," 1983.** Michael Hayes and James D. Papa writing the first proprietary wrestling theme — also the first wrestling music video — and the long legal tail that came twenty-nine years later.

15. **"Real American" was written for somebody else.** Rick Derringer and Bernard Kenny wrote it for the U.S. Express; the song got reassigned to Hogan because the company didn't want the production investment to walk out the door. (Verify per #7.)

16. **Stone Cold's broken glass.** Jim Johnston layering car-crash audio + explosion + deep-bass note + glass shatter to give the stinger weight. The three-second sound design that defined an era.

17. **The Undertaker's funeral organ.** Johnston using church bells, organ, and a tempo slow enough to force the arena to settle into gravitas — "Phantom of the Opera" as wrestling theme.

18. **"No Chance in Hell" as office grievance.** Johnston channeling his own workplace resentment into McMahon's heel anthem. Subtext made text.

19. **Papa v. WWE, 2012.** WWE allegedly re-registering Papa's BMI catalog with Jim Johnston listed as composer instead. The lawsuit that revealed how the auditory archive becomes a balance-sheet asset.

20. **The Network sanitization.** What got dubbed over and why — "Enter Sandman" stripped from Sandman's ECW *One Night Stand* entrance, "Voodoo Child" pulled from Hollywood Hogan WCW footage. The cost of streaming history.

21. **The ambient-microphone loophole.** Pre-1985 promoters arguing that music played over the arena PA, picked up "ambiently" by TV mics, didn't need broadcast licenses. Held until ASCAP and BMI noticed cable wrestling money.

22. **The 1985 crackdown.** ASCAP and BMI catching up to wrestling's national expansion and forcing the in-house composer model that Johnston and Hart filled.

## Gaps the longform doesn't cover

Items the user flagged in the brief plus others I'd surface:

- **Lucha libre tradition.** Mil Máscaras gets one paragraph in Tab 2 (and only via his AJPW use, not Mexico). EMLL/CMLL tradition of mariachi-led entrances, El Santo's mariachi-card walks, Atlantis' theme, Blue Demon's organ entrance, the band-led tradition specific to Arena México and Arena Coliseo.
- **Women's wrestling music history beyond Mildred Burke.** Wendi Richter entering to Cyndi Lauper's "Girls Just Want to Have Fun" at *The Brawl to End It All* (July 1984) is arguably the single biggest entrance-music moment of the 1980s — it directly enabled *The Wrestling Album*. Sherri Martel's screeching in Savage's corner. Sapphire's "Time to Make the Donuts" theme. The GLOW theme song as a women's wrestling artifact.
- **NWA / JCP music tradition.** Dusty Rhodes entering to Tom Jones' "She's a Lady." Magnum T.A.'s synth theme. The Four Horsemen's Olympia-fanfare theme. The Crockett era had a different musical character than WWF and the doc skips it.
- **Memphis-specific tradition before Jimmy Hart went to WWF.** Hart producing for Lawler, Funk, Dundee. The Memphis tape-loop aesthetic.
- **Mid-South / Universal Wrestling Federation.** JYD entering to Queen's "Another One Bites the Dust" — possibly the most iconic single-song entrance of any territory. The doc mentions JYD only via the *Wrestling Album*.
- **WCW music — Power Plant through nWo era.** Sting's "Crow" theme. Goldberg's industrial theme. The nWo's cinematic theme. The doc references Hogan/"Voodoo Child" only as an example of Network sanitization.
- **AWA tradition.** Verne Gagne's territory had its own conventions. (Brian Ferguson's *Verne* book — now in the bibliography — likely covers this.)
- **Hawaiian / Polynesian Pacific.** The Polynesian Pro Wrestling tradition of using Hawaiian music with ukulele cues; haole-villain music selection; Peter Maivia's traditional Samoan entrances.
- **Smoky Mountain Wrestling 1991–1995.** Jim Cornette's territory was deliberately old-school, but the music game was strong — the Heavenly Bodies' theme, Tracy Smothers, Rock 'n' Roll Express revival.
- **ECW.** Gets a brief mention via Sandman/"Enter Sandman" and Harry Slash. The actual ECW music ethos — Pantera, White Zombie, public-domain industrial — deserves its own thread. WWE bought the ECW music catalog in January 2005 (the doc has the date but doesn't unpack it).
- **AEW and the modern proprietary-track era.** Mikey Rukus' AEW work. NXT's CFO$ era and what changed when CFO$ left WWE in 2020. The doc references this but doesn't develop it.
- **Howard Finkel and ring-announcer cadences.** Adjacent to music but the same auditory-framing function — Finkel's "And NEW…" is its own kind of stinger.
- **JBL / Wrestling rights buyouts.** When WWE bought ECW (2003) and WCW (2001) catalogs, what got included vs. excluded.
- **The pre-licensed "needle-drop" library era.** Before Johnston, many territories used royalty-free production music libraries (KPM, De Wolfe). Identifying which themes came from which library is a niche but rich angle.

## Structural notes on the doc itself

- The four-tab structure works for research but won't translate cleanly to a single longform — Tabs 3 and 4 (licensing economics) are useful background but heavy reading for a magazine feature audience. Recommend sidebars or a follow-up piece on licensing specifically.
- Footnote density is good but the *quality* of footnotes is uneven. Roughly half the citations are Wikipedia or fandom wikis, which won't survive scrutiny if WFIA wants to position this as serious history. Worth a citation cleanup pass.
- The doc reads well in tone — direct, plain language, no LinkedIn voice. Some sentences run long ("auditory sigil," "Pavlovian anchor") but that's the writer's register; not a bug.
